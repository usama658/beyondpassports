## 1. Question presented
1. Does Dubai DET activity 8299016 "Visa Services", any emirate tourism-licence requirement, or the federal Commercial Companies Law foreign-company rule apply to a UK company with no UAE presence selling this service online to UAE residents?
2. Do UAE consumer-protection and e-commerce rules (Federal Law 15/2020 as amended by 5/2023; Cabinet Decision 66/2023; Federal Decree-Law 14/2023; TDRA) impose obligations (Arabic, VAT-inclusive display, refund terms) on a foreign online seller to UAE consumers?
3. UAE VAT: does a non-resident supplier of consultancy services to UAE consumers have a registration obligation under Federal Decree-Law 8/2017, and what does that imply for AED price display?
4. UAE PDPL (Federal Decree-Law 45/2021): notice, lawful-basis, retention and cross-border conditions for passport and bank data of UAE residents processed in the UK?
5. Advertising law: constraints on visa-service advertising, "guarantee" language, Arabic requirements?
6. Would enquiry-only trip ideas (hotels + ground transport, no flights, no payment) constitute licensable tourism or travel-agency activity?

### 3c. VAT
- Federal Decree-Law 8/2017. u.ae (reached): mandatory registration when a UAE-based business exceeds AED 375,000; voluntary above 187,500; mandatory regardless of value "If a non UAE-based business makes taxable supplies in the UAE, regardless of its value, and there is no other person obligated to pay the due tax on these supplies in the UAE."
- FTA "Registration for VAT" (reached): "The mandatory registration threshold is AED 375,000. This threshold is not applicable to foreign businesses."
- PwC (secondary): "No registration threshold applies to non-resident businesses making supplies on which the UAE VAT is required to be charged."
- Place-of-supply articles (default + electronic-services exception) and tax-inclusive price-display article NOT reached [unverified]. The deep dive's "UAE displays VAT-inclusive" is practitioner observation.

### 3d. Personal data
- Federal Decree-Law 45/2021 PDPL. u.ae (reached): applies to processing "inside or outside the country"; "prohibits the processing of personal data without the consent of its owner, except for some cases"; sets cross-border transfer requirements; data subjects may request correction and restriction; in force "2 January 2022"; UAE Data Office handles complaints and guidelines.
- DLA Piper (secondary): scope extends to "External Controllers/Processors processing UAE Data Subjects' data"; Article 4 bases incl consent, contract performance, legal procedures, public interest; Article 13 notice must cover purposes, "sectors receiving data (UAE or external)", "cross-border transfer protection measures"; no general retention period; transfers limited to territories with "sufficient provisions, measures, controls"; DIFC/ADGM regimes intact; "As of January 6, 2025, Executive Regulations remain unpublished"; penalties await Cabinet decision. Verbatim Articles 2, 4, 13, 22, 23 [unverified]; current status of regulations and any UK adequacy decision [unverified].

## 4. Analysis, both readings
### Q1 Licence reach
Reading A (engaged): Decree-Law 14/2023 covers online sale of services and u.ae says online sellers must obtain licences; /ae/ pages + AED pricing + UAE-geo ads = sustained targeted selling; Cabinet Resolution 200/2025 shows enforcement machinery; a DET code exists for exactly this service, every competitor is licensed, and police tell consumers to use "licensed service providers" and check the National Economic Register, so the market treats the licence as the legitimacy test; "All businesses in the UAE must have a physical address".
Reading B (not engaged): service performed entirely in the UK, no act in the UAE; CCL foreign-company rule targets carrying on activity in the State (wording unverified); Consumer Protection Law expressly carves out "eCommerce businesses registered outside the UAE", so the legislature contemplates foreign online sellers existing without UAE registration; the DET code regulates Dubai licensees, not foreign remote sellers; u.ae licensing statement addresses businesses setting up in the UAE.
Indication: Medium to High uncertainty; deciding facts = Article 2 of 14/2023 and the CCL foreign-company article, neither readable. Practical enforcement against a UK entity most plausibly = TDRA site blocking, ad-platform complaints, consumer-complaint publicity (inference).

### Q2 Consumer/e-commerce obligations
Reading A: 14/2023 obligations described without residency limit; 200/2025 may define violations by reference to the UAE consumer; 42/2023 may apply to misleading advertising reaching UAE consumers. Reading B: u.ae states 15/2020 "does not apply to eCommerce ... businesses registered outside the UAE", so Arabic-invoice, Arabic-information, price-display and AED 2m penalties are not engaged; gap = 14/2023, 42/2023, 200/2025 scope unknown.
Indication: Low to Medium for 15/2020 itself; unknown for the others. Offering an Arabic summary of terms, clear refund policy and tax-transparent AED pricing costs little.

### Q3 VAT
Reading A: non-UAE business making taxable supplies in the UAE must register "regardless of its value" where no other person is obligated (u.ae; FTA; PwC); B2C clients are not registered, no reverse charge; if place of supply is the UAE, register before first sale, charge 5%, file, display tax-inclusive. Reading B: GCC default places services at the supplier's residence with exceptions (electronic services used in the UAE; B2B to registered recipients); human-delivered consultancy over WhatsApp/email is not "electronic services" (automated, minimal human intervention); BP makes no UAE taxable supply and cannot register or charge UAE VAT; AED price = UK supply priced in AED. Articles [unverified].
Price-display consequence: under A "AED X incl. 5% VAT" + UAE TRN; under B displaying "incl. VAT" would itself be misleading; honest display "AED X total, no UAE VAT charged; Beyond Passports Ltd is a UK company" with UK VAT position stated separately. THE DEEP DIVE'S "AED 649 INCL. 5%" PRESUPPOSES READING A AND MUST NOT BE LOCKED UNTIL SETTLED. UK side: B2C services to non-UK consumers generally take the supplier's location for UK VAT; refer to the UK accountant.
Indication: Medium; binary on the place-of-supply article.

### Q4 PDPL
Reading A: applies in full (u.ae "inside or outside the country"; DLA "External Controllers"); BP needs lawful basis (consent default; contract performance alternative), Article 13 notice, defined retention (30 days must be stated and honoured), lawful transfer route; UK adequacy decision not found, so Article 23 routes (contract, consent) likely needed. Reading B: applies but not yet operationally enforceable (regulations unpublished Jan 2025; penalties await Cabinet; no Data Office adequacy/transfer mechanisms found); UK GDPR compliance with ICO registration, lawful-basis record, privacy notice and deletion schedule substantively covers the summarised duties.
Indication: Medium; clearly extraterritorial on primary text, enforcement framework incomplete.

### Q5 Advertising
Reading A: 55/2023 covers digital publishing by "individuals, organisations" in the UAE; UAE-geo ads are published to the UAE; TDRA treats "false or inaccurate information" as prohibited; permit regime shows active licensing with fines to AED 500,000; consumer/fraud laws penalise misleading prices; "guaranteed appointment"/"fast-track" = paradigm misleading claim. Reading B: Media Council says no permit when "only promoting their own business or project"; permit presupposes a UAE licence (aimed at licensed influencers/agencies); 55/2023 framed around media activities "in the UAE"; Consumer Protection Law excludes foreign e-commerce; no Arabic-language advertising requirement found (Arabic duties found are invoice/product-information for UAE-registered sellers); BP's copy rules already ban guarantee/fast-track language and Google Ads Government Documents policy is the operative constraint.
Indication: Low to Medium, content-driven not permit-driven. Open: whether 55/2023 or permit rules reach foreign advertisers; Law 15/1980 status.

### Q6 Trip ideas
Reading A: if Dubai treats "organising or marketing tours" as the licensed act, itineraries + "enquire" button = marketing tour packages; competitors hold DTCM/DET licences for the bundle. Reading B: no price, booking, payment or contract with BP; editorial content with a conversation starter; fulfilment by a licensed operator with BP as introducer; licensing regimes typically attach to selling/arranging for consideration.
Indication: Medium uncertainty, Low practical exposure while truly enquiry-only. Any step toward quoting prices, deposits or confirming bookings flips to A and also engages UK PTR 2018.

## 6. What the UAE attorney needs to resolve
1. Decree-Law 14/2023 Article 2 + Cabinet Resolution 200/2025: does it apply to a trader established outside the UAE with no licence selling services online to UAE consumers? Which obligations, which authority?
2. CCL 32/2021: quote the foreign-company article; does remote cross-border supply with no office, agent, staff or local payment collection = "carrying on activity in the State"?
3. DET activity 8299016: obtain the activity sheet; does it carry DET Tourism external approval; DET's position on remote foreign providers; same for Abu Dhabi DCT.
4. VAT place of supply by article for a UK-established supplier's human-delivered consultancy to a non-registered UAE individual; can WhatsApp/email delivery be "electronic services"; tax-inclusive display rule; what a non-registered foreign supplier should display in AED.
5. PDPL: executive regulations and penalties status; any Data Office UK adequacy decision; contract performance vs explicit consent for passport/bank data; minimum Article 13 notice; defensibility of 30-day post-decision retention.
6. Advertising: is Law 15/1980 repealed by 55/2023; instrument creating the 2025 permit and confirmation it does not apply to a foreign company advertising its own services; no Arabic rule for consumer-facing digital advertising by a foreign advertiser.
7. Tourism: identify the Dubai travel-agent/tour-operator instrument; is publishing unpriced, enquiry-only itineraries with no booking or payment a licensable act?
8. Fallbacks: can a free-zone e-commerce/consultancy licence lawfully serve mainland consumers for this activity; does the Dubai Virtual Company Licence permit "visa services"/consultancy; realistic all-in first-year cost of each.
