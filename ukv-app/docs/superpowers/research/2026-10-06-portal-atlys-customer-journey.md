# Atlys customer journey: end-to-end portal experience for a Schengen visa

Research date: 2026-10-06. Lane: Atlys only (atlys.com and market sub-paths). Purpose: ground Beyond Passports' customer-portal decision in what Atlys customers actually see, are promised, and complain about.

Every claim carries its source URL and the date it was seen. Anything not directly verified is tagged [unverified]. Review excerpts are quoted short, with reviewer handle, star rating and date as shown on the source page. Where a page geo-redirected or returned 404 that is stated.

Method note: atlys.com/uk, /uk/en-gb/schengen-visa, /terms-of-service and /refund-policy all returned 404; atlys.com/en-GB and /en-GB/visa/france-visa geo-redirected to the Indonesia locale (en-ID). No UK-locale homepage or GBP price page could be retrieved from this environment. GBP amounts in this report come from UK reviewers, not from an Atlys price page. Google Play could not be fetched directly (content truncated twice, 403 on AppBrain); Play figures come from the apkcombo mirror.

## 1. Entry: landing page to account

**Discovery.** The homepage is a destination search with filters for "visa delivery time, type, document requirements, and holidays"; headline "Get Visas On Time to 120+ Countries - Guaranteed On-Time Delivery"; a "My Profile" link is the route to login; a "Live Video Call" link reads "Join a live video call with our expert to get help with your visa application". https://www.atlys.com/en-US [seen 2026-10-06]

**Country product page (Schengen example, Austria, Indian-resident locale).** Shows consular fee "€90" adult, "€45" children 6-12, "Free" under 6, with the caveat "Fees may vary slightly due to exchange rate fluctuations and additional service charges from visa appointment centres". A 9-step "how it works" (form, appointment booking, VAC submission, biometrics, payment, passport delivery). Promises "a completed application packet with all necessary documents" including cover letter, flight reservations, hotel reservations, day-to-day itineraries, travel insurance, sponsorship/invitation letters. "Atlys Protect (free)" badge. FAQ: "No Visa Slots Available Right Now" with slot alerts. The Atlys fee vs VAC fee split is NOT itemised on the page. https://www.atlys.com/en-US/visa/austria-visa [seen 2026-10-06]

## 2. Payment

**When.** Payment is taken before document review and before submission; the Terms cancel the application if the customer does not "revert to the communications sent by Atlys within 24 hours of the receipt" (Section 4.2(iii)). https://www.atlys.com/en-US/terms [seen 2026-10-06]. Native checkout with Apple Pay (17 Jun 2026) and Adyen (8 Jun 2026) shipped. https://www.atlys.com/speed [seen 2026-10-06]

**"Pay When On Time" model.** Transparency report 2025 (dated 24 Feb 2026): "You're only charged the service fee when your visa is approved and delivered on time." https://www.atlys.com/transparency-2025 [seen 2026-10-06]. In practice UK customers describe this as a deferred fee that is not understood at checkout: "the 'processing fee' showed as £145 - £0 for now, which clearly made it look like nothing else was due... atlys suddenly demanded £144.67 to 'unlock' it" (Cass, GB, 1 star, 5 Nov 2025). https://uk.trustpilot.com/review/atlys.com?search=%22for%20now%22 [seen 2026-10-06]

**Itemisation.** Blog promise for Schengen: "Embassy fee, service charges, and Atlys fee combined upfront. No add-on charges." https://www.atlys.com/blog/schengen-visa (26 May 2026) [seen 2026-10-06]. Third-party observation: "Atlys processing fee" typically "$10-$50" plus "Government fee: Set by the destination country, paid through Atlys on your behalf". https://localsinsider.com/apps/visa-online-application-app-atlys-review-prices/ (17 Sep 2026) [seen 2026-10-06]. Atlys's own fee blog explains consular €90, VAC "approximately €22", optional add-ons (courier, SMS, prime-time), but "does not disclose what Atlys itself charges". https://www.atlys.com/blog/schengen-visa-fees (10 Oct 2025) [seen 2026-10-06]. Indian pricing for France: appointment fee "about ₹2,047" and service fee "₹2,950". https://www.business-standard.com/immigration/visas-for-just-1-atlys-uk-schengen-uae-deal-live-on-august-4-5-125073001197_1.html [seen 2026-10-06]. GBP price page for UK residents: not retrievable [unverified].

**Currency per market.** Government fee standardised in USD with "$1 = ₹84" for India. https://www.atlys.com/transparency [seen 2026-10-06]. Irish reviewer: "having said we were from Ireland we still had to pay in sterling not EURO" https://uk.trustpilot.com/review/atlys.com?page=3 [seen 2026-10-06]. Credits are INR-denominated: "No matter what currency you purchase, credits are in INR" (Kalpesh Hegde, 1 star, May 2026) https://uk.trustpilot.com/review/atlys.com?search=credits [seen 2026-10-06]. Conversion complaint: "You have to pay conversion rates" (Van, AU, 1 star, 31 Aug 2026) https://uk.trustpilot.com/review/atlys.com?stars=1&page=2 [seen 2026-10-06]. A "price change log" exists: "Every fee ever changed is right here. With time and reason." https://www.atlys.com/transparency/price-change-log [seen 2026-10-06]. FX promise: "We refund the difference" when the rate drops between payment and submission. https://www.atlys.com/transparency-2025 [seen 2026-10-06]

**Guarantees, exact wording.**
- On Time Guarantee page: "We deliver your visa exactly as promised, and we guarantee it." "If we are unable to deliver your visa within the promised timeframe, we offer you a 100% refund. We also give you the visa when it arrives." Timing is derived from "data points from past visa timelines, insights from our PRO team, and factors such as seasonal variations and embassy holidays". The page does not state which fee is refunded, which routes are covered, or how to claim. https://www.atlys.com/on-time-guaranteed [seen 2026-10-06]
- App store copy: "We are 99.7% on time. What if we miss? We give you a full refund. No questions asked." https://apps.apple.com/gb/app/atlys-visas-on-time/id1514036839 [seen 2026-10-06]
- Refunds policy (stage-based): not yet filed = "100% refund", "issued as Atlys credits instantly"; after submission "it enters an irreversible process and refunds are no longer possible"; rejected on an eligible route = "100% refund of the total fee (government fee and Atlys service fee)" under AtlysProtect; approved but late = "the Atlys service fee is waived"; bank transfers "up to 5 working days". AtlysProtect is "automatically included at no extra cost for eligible destinations". Eligible routes are not listed on the page. https://www.atlys.com/transparency/refunds-policy [seen 2026-10-06]
- Claim path (from search snippet of the same policy page): "Navigate to your application, tap 'Request Refund'... The team reviews requests within 24 hours. Refunds are processed within 5 business days of approval." [unverified: snippet only]
- Terms override: "Atlys will not refund the Visa fees or the Atlys fees for any reason whatsoever, except in cases which are covered under 'Atlys Protect' as displayed at the time of application" and "even if your visa is rejected by the embassy/consulate... Atlys will not be liable to offer you a refund" (Section 5.3); "No refund of the Atlys service fee or the visa fee will be issued in such cases" (5.5); the ETA or appointment slot "is indicative and is on the best effort basis... and not binding" (2.8). https://www.atlys.com/en-US/terms [seen 2026-10-06]
- Narrower AtlysProtect wording elsewhere: "if your supported visa is denied under qualifying conditions, you get a refund on the Atlys service fee" (service fee only, not total). https://www.atlys.com/blog/schengen-visa (26 May 2026) and https://www.atlys.com/blog/schengen-visa-rejection-reasons (1 Jun 2026) [seen 2026-10-06]. Press announcement of AtlysProtect dated 6 Feb 2026: "a 100% refund if a visa application is denied under qualifying conditions"; covers "eVisas, United States, Schengen, and Passport Collection". https://traveltrade.today/travel-startups/notable-travel-startups/atlys-mitigates-visa-rejection-risk-announces-transparent-refund-policy/ [seen 2026-10-06]

Contradiction to note: the refunds page says 100% of total fee on rejection; two Atlys blogs say Atlys service fee only; the Terms say no refund except as "displayed at the time of application". Customers report discovering the Protect condition only at claim time: "'You need to have AtlysProtect,' which you cannot find anywhere...no refund" (Manool, PL, 1 star, 13 Sep 2026). https://uk.trustpilot.com/review/atlys.com?stars=1 [seen 2026-10-06]

**Add-ons sold.** Travel insurance (a UK partner named by one reviewer as HOOD), flight/hotel reservations, courier. "The Atlys site/app has an offer of travel insurance with a UK company (HOOD). DO NOT BUY!!" (Claudette McKay, 1 star, 23 Jan 2026) https://uk.trustpilot.com/review/atlys.com?search=insurance [seen 2026-10-06]; "added bonus of travel insurance under one roof" (JULIAN HENRY, 5 stars, 28 Jun 2026) same URL.

## Gaps Beyond Passports can exploit

Ranked by how often the underlying pain appears in the review corpus above. Each is a customer-facing feature or promise Atlys does not deliver or delivers badly.

1. **A named human case owner, reachable on WhatsApp and phone, with a published reply SLA (for example "every message answered by a person within 2 working hours, 9-7 UK").** Atlys routes to BOLO/Tars AI (38% of interactions by their own count) and customers say "You can't even talk to a human" and "only AI robot Rahul will talk". BP already sells human handling; make it a portal promise with a visible owner name, photo and last-reply timestamp on every case. Source pain: Section 7, item 1.

2. **Cash refunds to the original payment method, stated before payment, never store credit.** Atlys refunds pre-filing cancellations "as Atlys credits instantly" and customers report "refused and would only offer credits" and INR-denominated wallets. BP promise: "Any refund goes back to the card you paid with within 5 working days. We do not issue credits." Source pain: Section 7, item 2.

3. **One locked GBP price at checkout, itemised consular fee / VAC fee / BP fee, with "nothing more to pay" in writing.** Atlys shows "£0 for now" then charges on delivery, and quotes shift when residence country changes. BP's £40 + £90 structure is already fixed; the portal should display the full £130 and the government and VAC amounts separately, and state that the VAC fee is paid at the centre, before the customer pays anything. Source pain: Section 7, item 3.

4. **No "guaranteed by" date. Instead, dated milestones anchored to the real appointment plus a public change log per case.** Atlys's own Terms call the ETA "indicative... not binding" while the app shows a "Guaranteed by" date that customers watch move. BP should show: appointment date (with VAC reference), consulate's published processing window, and expected passport-return window, and log every change with a reason, pushed to WhatsApp. Source pain: Section 7, item 4.

5. **Event-based status with evidence, not synthetic stages.** Atlys states show "Under Processing" while nothing has been submitted, and automated WhatsApps claim background checks after the passport has shipped. BP statuses should each be tied to a real artefact the customer can open: documents reviewed (by whom, when), appointment confirmed (VAC confirmation PDF), submitted at VAC (receipt), decision received, passport returned (courier reference). Source pain: Section 7, item 5.

6. **Human-checked application pack delivered at least 3 working days before the appointment, with a customer review-and-approve step.** Atlys packs arrive D-1 with AI cover letters containing wrong sex, dates and hotels. BP already produces hand-filled forms to a locked format; add a portal "Review your pack" screen where the customer confirms each field before the pack is final, and show who checked it. Source pain: Section 7, item 6.

7. **No dummy or unverifiable bookings. Ever.** Multiple Schengen refusals (Greece) are blamed on Atlys's "dummy or draft bookings"; a reviewer says Atlys "made forged docs for europe visa". BP promise: itinerary built only from the customer's own refundable or confirmed bookings, with a plain explanation of why consulates verify them. This is a compliance differentiator as well as a conversion one. Source pain: Section 7, item 6.

8. **Per-document checklist with explicit accept/reject reasons and a no-repeat rule.** Atlys customers are asked for the same passport pages "5-6 times" and are told a document is pending "without mentioning which". BP portal: each document has a state (needed, received, checked OK, needs re-upload with the specific reason), one reviewer, and uploads accepted by web, WhatsApp or email and attached to the case by the team. Source pain: Section 7, item 7.

9. **Honest UK appointment picture: name the real operator per country (TLScontact, VFS, BLS, GVCW), show which centre (London, Manchester, Edinburgh), and confirm with the VAC reference.** Atlys's UK availability page names only VFS and promises slots are "held" while its Terms say slots are "not binding"; customers report "no appointment confirmation" and cancellations they were never told about. BP already holds nearest-in-country appointments; surface the confirmation document and centre address in the portal, and notify on any change. Source pain: Section 7, item 8.

10. **Works on desktop and mobile web with email or phone OTP; no forced app install; no third-party OTPs.** Atlys customers cannot log in on a laptop, are forced into the app to continue, and were asked to put family members' WhatsApp numbers through OTP. BP portal: responsive web first, magic-link or OTP by email or SMS, applicant-only verification. Source pain: Section 7, item 9.

11. **UK legal footing shown on every portal page.** Atlys contracts as Atlys Inc. (Delaware), privacy controller "Atlas Visa, Inc." in San Francisco, with no UK GDPR language, no DPO and an App Store label claiming no data is collected. BP shows Companies House 17331903, England-and-Wales law, a UK GDPR privacy notice with retention periods for passport data, and (once in place) the ICO registration. Source: Section 6.

12. **Insurance kept separate and compliant, not bundled or upsold inside the visa flow.** Atlys bundles insurance (a UK reviewer names HOOD and calls it a scam) and customers say promised insurance was never arranged. BP should refer customers to buy their own compliant policy, and the portal should check the certificate meets the €30,000 Schengen minimum, in line with the FCA flag already recorded. Source pain: Section 7, item 10.

Things Atlys does well that BP should match rather than beat: passport-scan autofill, a visible stage timeline, and WhatsApp updates at each milestone (Section 8). Everything in the gaps list above is a promise Atlys makes in marketing and withdraws in its Terms; BP's advantage is making the promise once, in plain words, and keeping it.
