# iVisa customer journey research (portal benchmark)

Date of research: 2026-10-06. Every URL below was read on that date unless a different "seen" date is given. Scope is iVisa only (ivisa.com, help.ivisa.com, iVisa apps, third-party review sites about iVisa). Anything that could not be read directly is marked [unverified].

Purpose: feed the Beyond Passports customer portal decision (owner rule: competition-led, customer-centric). iVisa is the e-visa reference portal; its Schengen (embassy appointment) product is a smaller line sold at "From $399.99" with the government fee paid separately at the appointment.

Method notes

- ivisa.com main Schengen hub pages (/schengen-visa, /schengen) returned 404 on 2026-10-06; country pages (/switzerland/schengen-visa, /denmark/schengen-visa, /spain/schengen-visa) were readable.
- Help centre is Intercom-based (help.ivisa.com). Collection index pages 404'd; individual articles were readable.
- Trustpilot was read via the public listing and keyword-filtered views. Trustpilot shows relative dates for very recent reviews ("1 day ago"); those are recorded as 2026-10-05 or 2026-10-06 accordingly.
- Google Play listing could not be rendered by the fetch tool; its rating is taken from search-result snippets and is marked [unverified].

## 1. Entry: landing page to account

Eligibility check first, then product, then form.

- The visa checker asks nationality and destination, then shows whether a visa is needed and which options exist; the user then picks visa type and processing speed with price and time differences shown. Source: https://travelsloth.de/en/review-ivisa/ (independent review, Feb 2026), https://hostelgeeks.com/ivisa-reviews-evisa/ (updated 29.04.2026).
- Schengen country pages describe a three-step flow: "Complete the application" by "answering questions, uploading documents, selecting processing speed, and paying"; "We review and submit your application"; "Receive your ... Schengen Visa" by email. Claimed form time: "25 minutes Average time it takes to apply". Source: https://www.ivisa.com/switzerland/schengen-visa and https://ivisa.com/denmark/schengen-visa.
- No guest checkout in the usual sense: payment creates the account. "After they have received payment, an account is generated." Source: https://www.girlabouttheglobe.com/my-ivisa-review/ (seen 2026-10-06, review dated 25 Jun 2024). hostelgeeks lists "Payment required before submission" as a con. Source: https://hostelgeeks.com/ivisa-reviews-evisa/.
- Login page wording: "Check your order status or log in below", fields "Order Number or Email" and "Password", links "Forgot Password", "Get a password free login link", "Sign up for free". Source: https://www.ivisa.com/login.
- Passwordless fallback: entering a wrong password triggers an email "Here's your automatic login link". Source: search snippet of https://help.ivisa.com/en/articles/4626045-forgot-your-password [unverified: article returned 404 when fetched directly].
- Processing speed tiers (e-visas): Standard "3 business days", Rush "1 business day", Super Rush "8 hours, weekdays only". Source: https://hostelgeeks.com/ivisa-reviews-evisa/. Help centre on upgrades: "Only eVisas are eligible for upgrades"; "Only orders in Incomplete, Received, or Reviewed status can be upgraded"; "Upgrades speed up iVisa review times; but government processing times stay the same". Upgrade path in the account: "Need it sooner?" link under the order number. Source: https://help.ivisa.com/en/articles/9786479-how-to-upgrade-to-get-my-travel-document-sooner.
- Schengen pages do not show speed tiers. Switzerland page: standard processing "15 calendar days from the moment your application reaches the Switzerland Consulate", "up to 45 days during peak seasons", and the headline "Approved in as fast as 45 days"; advice to apply "at least 2 months before your trip". Source: https://www.ivisa.com/switzerland/schengen-visa.
- What is asked before payment (per independent walk-throughs): nationality, destination, visa type, speed, personal data, passport scan, photo where required; card or PayPal at checkout with immediate confirmation email. Source: https://travelsloth.de/en/review-ivisa/.
- App: "passport scanning with OCR technology that transfers information directly to your travel visa application", "real-time alerts", saved profile for faster repeat applications, "Expert review of applications before submission". Developer "Document Advisor LLC". Latest release v6.2.2 dated Sep 28 (2026). Source: https://apps.apple.com/us/app/ivisa-eta-evisa-nzeta-visa/id1053424792.
- Checkout add-ons that users report being surprised by: Denial Protection, iVisa+ subscription, "Embassy Registration fee" (see sections 2 and 7).

## 2. Payment

What is itemised

- Terms: "we will present the cost for the Service(s) you select along with any applicable government fee, tax and shipping." Source: https://www.ivisa.com/terms-and-conditions.
- Schengen pages: price "From $399.99" described as "our service fee, expert review, and support", followed by "no surprises"; the government fee is "Paid separately at the biometric appointment"; "A visa service center such as VFS, TLS or BLS may add its own fee". Source: https://www.ivisa.com/switzerland/schengen-visa. Denmark page shows the same "From $399.99" with no separate government or VAC fee itemised and no speed tiers. Source: https://ivisa.com/denmark/schengen-visa.
- So for Schengen, iVisa does not pass the embassy fee through; the customer pays it at the VAC. For e-visas, the government fee is bundled into the checkout total (reviews below consistently compare the iVisa total with the government price).
- Currency: prices are shown in USD on US-facing pages; reviewers quote GBP, EUR and AUD totals, so localised currency display exists [unverified: not tested directly].

iVisa+ subscription

- "Unlimited e-Visas, health declarations, and embassy registrations without any standard fees", "50% off of embassy appointment visas", "24/7 prioritized customer support". Individual $79.99 per year, Family (up to 5 people) $119.99 per year. "renew automatically, unless you cancel before the new billing cycle." Excludes government fees, passport renewal products, Denial Protection fees, other add-ons. Source: https://www.ivisa.com/plus.
- Terms: charges occur "during the first month of each subsequent renewal period" unless cancelled; "Subscription cancellations and downgrades will be applied to the next billing cycle." Source: https://www.ivisa.com/terms-and-conditions.
- Cancel path: Account > name menu > "iVisa+" > "Manage Plan" > toggle "Want to cancel your plan?". Source: https://help.ivisa.com/en/articles/8807393-how-to-manage-my-ivisa-subscription.
- Subscription refund: "You get a 100% refund if you request it within 30 days of buying iVisa+ and have not used any benefits"; 7-day window for renewals. Source: https://help.ivisa.com/en/articles/6112152-the-ivisa-refund-policy.

Refund policy (exact wording)

- "You can't get a refund if the order was created more than 3 months ago or, the order has been inactive for 3 months". "Allow up to 3 business days for us to process your refund, and up to 14 business days (Monday to Friday) for it to appear in your account." Source: https://help.ivisa.com/en/articles/6112152-the-ivisa-refund-policy.
- E-visas: 100% while Incomplete/Received; 0% once "Waiting on government" or Complete/Rejected. iVisa blog adds: under internal review "you can get 75% of the iVisa processing fee back". Source: https://www.ivisa.com/blog/ivisa-refund-policy-what-youre-actually-entitled-to (published 16 Jun 2026, updated 9 Jul 2026).
- Appointment-required visas (Schengen): 100% at early stages, "50% once passed to immigration partner", "no refund if sent for final review" / 0% once the appointment is scheduled or complete. Sources: same two pages.
- Governing rule: "Once iVisa submits your application to a government authority, the fees you paid cannot be refunded." "A rejection doesn't automatically mean a refund. If the government turns down your application, you won't get the fees back, unless you purchased Denial Protection when you applied." Source: https://www.ivisa.com/blog/ivisa-refund-policy-what-youre-actually-entitled-to.
- How to request: "Connect with us on our WhatsApp, chat (select the green icon on the bottom right of the screen) or send an email to help@ivisatravel.com"; enter a 6-digit verification code; "Provide your reason for requesting a refund, and our chatbot will automatically generate your refund". Source: https://help.ivisa.com/en/articles/9335328-how-to-request-a-refund.

Approval promises and exclusions

- No approval guarantee. Terms: "there is no guarantee that the use of the Service and our products will assure you of obtaining the documentation required for your desired travel." Processing times "are estimates and not guarantees." Liability cap: "iVISA SHALL BE LIABLE ONLY TO THE EXTENT OF ACTUAL DAMAGES INCURRED BY YOU, NOT TO EXCEED U.S.$100." Source: https://www.ivisa.com/terms-and-conditions.
- Denial Protection add-on: "guarantees a full refund if your application is rejected by the government." Refunds "Government fees, Service fees, Standard processing fees, Rush processing fees, Add-ons, Subscription fees"; the Denial Protection fee itself is non-refundable; must be bought at checkout, cannot be added later, one per applicant, only the rejected applicant is refunded, automatic refund with email notice, up to 14 business days. Price not stated in the article. Source: https://help.ivisa.com/en/articles/13171800-denial-protection. Reviewer-quoted prices: "Charged €34 denial protection fee" (Spiros Andriotis, 3 stars, 14 May 2025) and "£14.84 Denial Protection fee" (Fru Lorna Österlund, 1 star, 3 Apr 2025). Source: https://uk.trustpilot.com/review/ivisa.com?search=denial%20protection.
- Marketing: "99% Applicant approval rate" defined as "Completed iVisa orders divided by completed plus rejected orders", "An internal measure, not an official government statistic". Source: https://www.ivisa.com/why-use-ivisa. The iVisa+ page says "98% visa approval rate" and "1.4M satisfied customers" and "10 years operating experience" (inconsistent with 99%, 3M+, founded 2013 elsewhere). Source: https://www.ivisa.com/plus.

## 3. Document upload

- Flow: "Near the end of your application form, you'll upload your documents" via "Add document" or "Add photo"; upload from desktop, by scanning a QR code with a phone, or by email; then "Submit your application". After submission: "You'll receive an email confirming that we're reviewing your application to ensure it's government-compliant." The article does not state accepted formats, size limits, who reviews, or what happens on rejection. Source: https://help.ivisa.com/en/articles/4707459-how-to-upload-documents.
- Rejected-upload loop is handled by the status "Action required: Provide more information": "We require more information from you... An email detailing the questions and explanations has been sent." Source: https://help.ivisa.com/en/articles/4624872-how-to-check-the-status-of-my-order.
- Schengen document list shown on product pages: passport valid 3+ months beyond departure, digital photos, travel insurance with €30,000 minimum cover, recent bank statements (Switzerland page cites "typically €105/day"; Denmark "€40 to €70 daily"), flight and accommodation reservations, proof of employment or student status, email address. Sources: https://www.ivisa.com/switzerland/schengen-visa, https://ivisa.com/denmark/schengen-visa.
- Photo checking: "iVisa combines country-specific guidance, an AI compliance check, and human review before submission"; the AI flags "poor lighting, incorrect dimensions, improper framing, and formatting problems"; fulfilment staff "catch and fix issues like shadows or head tilts"; "your photo is reviewed as part of your application process, so no time is lost". Source: https://www.ivisa.com/blog/ivisa-photo-guarantee-get-your-visa-photo-right (published 1 Jun 2026, modified 11 Aug 2026).
- Standalone passport photo product: $8.29 digital, $16.99 printed, with human review. Source: search snippet from https://onedollarpassportphoto.com/ivisa-photos-alternative/ [unverified: ivisa.com/passport-photos returned 404]. Third-party app reviewers note you "can't preview your completed passport photo until after you've paid". Source: search snippet citing https://photoaid.com/blog/free-passport-photo-apps/ [unverified].
- Who reviews: Switzerland page claims "UK-certified immigration consultants review every application". Source: https://www.ivisa.com/switzerland/schengen-visa. No registration body or number is shown for this claim [unverified].
- What the customer gets back: e-visas are emailed and "Users can download documents directly from their iVisa account". Schengen: "you will receive your passport back with the visa sticker attached... You can also find a copy of your Schengen Visa confirmation in your iVisa dashboard". Sources: https://help.ivisa.com/en/articles/4624872-how-to-check-the-status-of-my-order, https://www.ivisa.com/switzerland/schengen-visa.
- Upload friction in reviews: "Their system auto-cropped it during upload and chopped off part of my chin and the top of my head" (Steven Morgan, 1 star, 11 Jul 2026); "The only difficult thing was related with the process to upload the personal photo. It requires 3 or 4 times" (Ernesto, 4 stars, 7 Sep 2026); "I didn't know I'd have to upload photos and I was using the PC and I struggled with this" (CDS, 3 stars, 21 Sep 2026). Source: https://uk.trustpilot.com/review/ivisa.com?search=upload. One complaint aggregator reports an applicant "asked to upload 47 passport pages individually". Source: https://www.sandspice.com/ivisa/ (undated opinion piece) [unverified].

## 4. Status tracking, notifications, appointments, delivery

Status states (verbatim from help centre)

1. "We're reviewing your application" ("You've completed the application successfully, and our team will begin processing it shortly.")
2. "Action required: Provide more information"
3. "The government is reviewing your application" ("Your application is submitted, and we're waiting on the government's response.")
4. "We're scheduling your appointment" ("We've reviewed your application and are actively securing the earliest available appointment for you.", not used for e-visas)
5. "Your documents are ready!" ("We've emailed the documents to you, and you're all set!")
Source: https://help.ivisa.com/en/articles/4624872-how-to-check-the-status-of-my-order. The refund policy also uses the internal states Incomplete, Received, Reviewed, Waiting on government, Complete, Rejected. Source: https://help.ivisa.com/en/articles/6112152-the-ivisa-refund-policy.

Notifications

- Help centre mentions only email ("check spam folders"). Source: https://help.ivisa.com/en/articles/4624872-how-to-check-the-status-of-my-order. App promises "real-time alerts" (push). Source: https://apps.apple.com/us/app/ivisa-eta-evisa-nzeta-visa/id1053424792. Spain page: "Application tracking notifications", "Support via WhatsApp, email, and chat". Source: https://www.ivisa.com/spain/schengen-visa. WhatsApp is a support channel; no evidence of proactive WhatsApp status pushes [unverified].
- Customers praise cadence: "You receive daily status updates" (Andre Kiesewetter, 5 stars, 23 Sep 2026); "I received status updates every day" (Anu, 5 stars, 31 Aug 2026). Source: https://uk.trustpilot.com/review/ivisa.com?search=status.
- Customers also report the status is opaque or wrong: "does not give government tracking IDs" (Zoya Mirza, 1 star, 2026-10-05); "I asked iVisa to provide me with the official visa application number reference so I could verify the application" and was refused (Maya, 1 star, 8 Sep 2026); "Your website still falsely displays a status of 'Under Government Review'" (PA Sarah Kamel, 1 star, 3 Aug 2026); "Internal status changed to 'reviewed' after only ~15 minutes and 'info needed' after ~24 minutes" (Roland Schroder, 1 star, 29 Sep 2026); "delivery estimate was silently changed to 4-day" (Tomas Drapal, 1 star, 2026-10-05). Sources: https://www.trustpilot.com/review/ivisa.com?stars=1, https://uk.trustpilot.com/review/ivisa.com?search=status.

Appointments (consulate visas)

No slot availability is displayed, no date is promised, and the appointment price upgrade is not itemised on the page. (Kevin Ferrone, 1 star, 19 Oct 2023); "She repeatedly contacted iVisa to explain she was not available on 14 September" (Tim, 1 star, 14 Sep 2026); "iVisa asked me to go Switzerland visa center in Ghana to battle it on my own" (Salisu Saani, 2 stars, 20 Feb 2026); "I paid £464 for Schengen visa and received no value for money" (Kwaku Nti, 1 star, 31 Dec 2023). Delivery

- E-visa: PDF by email plus download in account. Schengen: passport with sticker returned via consulate/VAC; iVisa's journey ends at "attend an in-person appointment for biometric collection". Source: https://www.ivisa.com/switzerland/schengen-visa. No passport-return or courier tracking feature found [unverified].

## Gaps Beyond Passports can exploit

Ranked by how often customers raise the underlying pain. Each item names the iVisa behaviour it beats and the evidence.

1. Total price on screen before any personal data, itemised into our fee, embassy fee (€90 adult) and VAC fee, with a "what you pay if you go direct" line. iVisa shows "From $399.99" with the embassy fee "paid separately" and reviewers say "You do not see what your purchase costs" (Vasanthi, 30 Sep 2026). Pain rank 1.
2. Honest speed language: "Express speeds our handling, not the consulate's decision", with the consulate's own 15 to 45 day window shown separately, and an automatic refund of the express fee if we miss our own handling date. iVisa's help centre admits "government processing times stay the same" but sells Rush and Super Rush that reviewers say were "quietly" downgraded (Tomas Drapal, 2026-10-05). Pain rank 2.
3. A published, stage-based refund ladder shown inside the dashboard next to the current stage, plus a human-approved refund in one message, no 6-digit chatbot ritual. iVisa's own flow is "our chatbot will automatically generate your refund" and customers report "too bad, too late, no refund for you" (Mark Dixon, 2026-10-02). Pain rank 3.
4. Named human caseworker from day one, reachable on WhatsApp, with a stated human reply target during UK hours and no bot gate. iVisa's top one-star theme after price is "do not expect to ever speak with a real person" (Lexa Wilson, 24 Sep 2026). BP already runs WhatsApp-first; make it a portal promise. Pain rank 4.
5. No subscriptions, no pre-selected add-ons, no "protection" upsell at checkout; one price, one receipt. iVisa+ auto-renews at $79.99 and reviewers report "snuck a hidden... subscription onto my bill" (Ava Jenkins, 3 Jul 2026). Pain rank 5.
6. Appointment transparency for Schengen: show the live slot picture per consulate (BP slot board), the exact booked date, time and VAC address in the portal, the VAC confirmation PDF attached, and calendar plus WhatsApp reminders. iVisa promises only "Our partners secure the earliest slot" and customers waited "4 months" or arrived to find "no appointment, name not on list" (Rajaram Chinnayan, 28 Apr 2024; Kerona, 18 Jul 2023). BP's pay-£90-only-when-booked structure is the proof point. Pain rank 6.
7. Show the real references: VAC booking reference, consulate application number and a copy of the submitted form in the dashboard, so the client can verify independently. iVisa "does not give government tracking IDs" and "refused to provide" the application number (Zoya Mirza, 2026-10-05; Maya, 8 Sep 2026). Pain rank 7.
8. Refusal handling without a paid add-on: free refusal-letter review, a plain explanation of the refusal ground (BP REFLOGIC), and a re-application discount, stated up front. iVisa charges a Denial Protection fee that reviewers say did not pay out ("I had paid for protection fee and they said refund is not possible", Mama Pot, 1 Oct 2026). Pain rank 8.
9. Document checklist with per-item status (missing, uploaded, checked, needs fix with reason) and upload preview with manual crop; photo check before payment. iVisa's help article gives no formats or rejection loop and reviewers report "auto-cropped... chopped off part of my chin" (Steven Morgan, 11 Jul 2026). Pain rank 9.
10. Passwordless login by email or WhatsApp code that works first time, and downloads that never fail or ask for "another fee". iVisa reviews: "password didn't work" (Delaney Kim, 3 Jul 2026); "would not download... wanted another fee" (Peter Simmons, 2026-10-05). Pain rank 10.
11. Verifiable UK credentials on every page: Companies House 17331903, ICO registration, named consultants, and no vague "UK-certified" wording. iVisa's Switzerland page claims "UK-certified immigration consultants review every application" with no register number shown [unverified]. Trust gap rather than a complaint count.
12. Journey continues past the appointment: passport collection or courier status, decision date logged, and a "what to do at the VAC" brief. iVisa's Schengen flow ends at "attend an in-person appointment for biometric collection" (Switzerland page) and has no passport-return tracking found [unverified]. Low complaint volume today but no competitor covers it.

Keep from iVisa (customers love it): the 25-minute guided form, phone passport scan to upload, daily status emails, WhatsApp as a channel, and a saved profile for repeat applicants.
