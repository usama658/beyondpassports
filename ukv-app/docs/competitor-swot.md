# CONVERSION COPY ANALYSIS (lp-copy-expert)

## 5 strongest lines in the market
1. **BP: "A Schengen refusal stays on your record for 5 years."** — loss aversion with a true, specific number; reframes purchase as insurance. Nobody else has a consequence line this concrete. Keep and defend.
2. **Flypass: "secure the slot first, build the file backwards."** — process-insight authority; the most stealable-sounding line in the market and closest to BP's actual TLS workflow.
3. **Breakout: "one missing signature sets your case back months."** — micro-fear: small vivid cause, large consequence, reader pictures themselves doing it.
4. **FlyFast: "From £90 No Hidden Fees" + "Refunded If No Appointment."** — price anchor + reversible risk in 9 words, refund conditioned on a controllable deliverable (compliant AND persuasive).
5. **xVisa: £30 + £90 = £120 split display.** — commitment laddering; £30 entry collapses the decision; showing arithmetic reads as honesty.

## 5 weakest
1. GoVisa "Congratulations! You may be eligible…" — lottery-scam register, fastest trust-killer (and the impression-share leader runs it = opportunity).
2. TourLoom "0+ clients, 0/5, 0% approval" — proof element actively disproving the headline.
3. schengenvisaconsultants "1.5M Happy Customers, 22 Years" — implausible, self-claimed trust is anti-trust.
4. Atlys "Guaranteed On-Time" — guarantees a consulate-controlled outcome; ASA complaint waiting; per-country H1s are SEO labels with no reader in the sentence.
5. EuroPath "EuroPath Travel is a Trusted London-Based…" — brand-first, reader-absent, self-awarded adjective.

## Patterns BP must NOT copy
- Congratulatory pre-qualification; any counter/stat not live-sourced from ukv.stats; outcome guarantees ("Guaranteed On-Time", "Get your visa"); self-awarded trust adjectives without a proof artifact; quasi-official ambiguity; CTA verbs implying we issue visas ("Get Visa"); fake urgency ("3 slots left" — France/TLS can only honestly say "calendar releases in batches, we take the earliest date").
